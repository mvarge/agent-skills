# Brazilian Portuguese Subtitle Style Guide

- Translate for natural Brazilian Portuguese, not word-for-word English.
- Preserve meaning, tone, humor, profanity level, names, and relationships.
- Keep lines readable. Aim for at most 42 characters per line where practical and avoid awkward one-word wraps.
- Preserve cue line breaks when they represent separate speakers or useful phrasing. Rewrap long English lines naturally in Portuguese.
- Use a hyphen (`-`) for simultaneous dialogue when the source does. Keep one dash per speaker line.
- Translate sound descriptions inside square brackets, for example `[door closes]` to `[porta se fecha]`. Keep non-dialogue descriptions bracketed.
- Preserve `<i>`, `</i>`, musical notes, and other subtitle markup. Translate lyrics while keeping their structure.
- Do not translate personal names, place names, or established titles unless the Brazilian Portuguese form is standard.
- Keep Japanese honorifics such as `-san`, `-kun`, and `-chan` when they carry social meaning. Do not add honorifics that are absent from the source.
- Preserve register. Formal speech should remain formal, casual speech casual, and child speech age-appropriate.
- Do not add translator notes, explanations, or punctuation that is not justified by the dialogue.
- A cue containing only a sound description is still a cue and must receive a translated sound description.
